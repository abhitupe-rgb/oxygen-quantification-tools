Distribution
When to use Pipeline mode:
• Within one hospital — modelling which wards or buildings on a single campus should be piped into the central O₂ system vs kept on cylinders. Distances in metres or hundreds of metres.
• Between very close facilities — e.g. teaching hospital with annexed clinics on the same plot.

For inter-facility distribution across kilometres, Transport mode is usually more realistic.
Planning & Baseline
Reliability — backup reserves at spokes
Hub-and-spoke networks have a single point of failure. If the hub goes down, all spokes lose supply unless they hold a backup reserve. The tool now estimates backup cost per spoke automatically from each facility's demand and accessibility (set in the spoke table). The two inputs below set the system-wide assumptions.
Seasonality
Seasonality affects two things: (1) the hub must be sized for peak demand, and (2) annual oxygen consumption is higher than flat-rate models suggest. Set to 0% if your context has no seasonal variation.
🛢 Convert from cylinder pricing — most facilities buy per cylinder, not per m³
Enter what a facility typically pays per cylinder refill. The tool calculates the equivalent $/m³.

For reference: J/D cylinder (6.8 m³) refill prices vary widely by country and procurement arrangement — from around $8 on government-contracted prices in some countries up to $50–$110+ on private retail prices (incl. transport, leasing, deposits). Use whatever the facility actually pays — there's no universal default.

⚠ Model assumptions — replace with real data before use in the field

  • PSA plant CAPEX: Flat input figure. Real plants scale non-linearly by capacity — get vendor quotes per tier.
  • Pipeline cost ($150/m default): Highly variable. Most important source of uncertainty in the model.
  • O₂ demand per bed: Using 35% average utilisation. Validate against actual facility data.
  • Hub downtime risk: Not modelled. A single hub failure affects all spokes. Consider a cylinder backup cost at each spoke.
  • Demand aggregation: Hub sized for aggregate average + buffer. Simultaneous surge across all spokes is less likely than individual peaks.
